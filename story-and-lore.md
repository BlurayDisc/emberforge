# Emberforge - Story and Lore

This file is the one story of the game, told in 10 chapters. One chapter is one Bracket and one town. It also holds the lore bible (the true history of the world), the cast, and the rules that keep the characters consistent.

`design.md` stays the source of truth for game rules. This file is the source of truth for story, names and tone. The short texts in `data/i18n/` (prologue, castle tales, town, monster and material lore) must agree with this file. Section 9 lists the old texts that must change when the story is built into the game.

Spoilers: this file tells the whole story, including every twist.

---

## 1. The story in one page

You are a nobody. A young smith (boy or girl, the player chooses the name) from a dead hamlet no one remembers. You come to Hollowbrook, the safest, kindest town in the Vales. You like the king. You like the people. When Princess Elowen is taken in the night, you accept the king's request: arm brave heroes and bring her home.

The road east leads through ten lands. Each land is more dangerous than the last, and each shows you a different face of greed. At first you explain everything away. Then you cannot. Around the middle of the road you learn that the safe haven was never safe by luck. It was bought, with the blood of every land you walk through, and with the princess herself. The king is a coward who sold his daughter. His people took the peace and asked no questions.

At three quarters of the story you hate the world. Then you tire of hating it. You see that your own heroes are greedy and loyal in the same breath, that a master smith sells to both sides and still makes honest steel, and that "the world is selfish" and "I will be decent anyway" can both be true. You stop asking the world to be good. You decide what you will be in it.

Then you reach Elowen. She is clever, funny, stubborn and kind, and she has seen the worst of the world and still jokes about it. With her, you start to love the world again. Together you carry a small fire to the end of the map. The story never says "I love you". Everything points to it.

**Theme:** growth is not learning that the world is good, and not learning that the world is bad. It is learning that the world is both, and choosing who you are anyway.

**Tone:** old-school fantasy on the surface (a king, a lost princess, a long road, a dragon at the end). Warm and funny in small moments. Honest about greed, never cruel for its own sake. No chosen one. The Smith is nobody, and that is the point.

## 2. Lore bible (the true history)

Players learn this in pieces. Chapter numbers show when each piece comes out.

### The Ember Forge and the Wyrm

- A thousand winters ago, the dwarf-smiths and the first Smith-Queen, **Maelis**, lit the **Ember Forge** on the cliffs of Worldsend, on top of the sleeping **Ancient Wyrm**. The Forge's fire binds the Wyrm. While the fire burns, the Wyrm sleeps and the world is warm.
- Maelis was nobody before the fire: a dwarf-smiths' apprentice with a hammer and a stubborn song. The fire answers a living hand with Maelis's blood, a hammer, and a song. All three are needed. (Ch4 hints, ch8 explains, ch10 proves.)
- The Forge needs a keeper. When the line of Smith-Queens grew thin, the dwarf-smiths let the fire sink to one last ember and hid the royal blood among plain people, so that the Wyrm's servants could not find it. This is the **Long Dark**. It was a hiding, not a defeat. (Ch4)
- The Wyrm dreams. Its cold breath does not make people evil. It strips away what hides their fear and greed. The nearer to Worldsend, the thinner the hiding. (Ch10 states this plainly.) Hollowbrook is far away, so its greed wears nice clothes.

### Blackspire and the Quiet Treaty

- About two hundred years ago a lord of the western Vales, **Aldric the First**, climbed to the Wyrm's court. The Wyrm speaks through a voice in the tower of **Blackspire**, which rose in one night. The "king nobody has seen" is this voice.
- The **Quiet Treaty** has three clauses:
  1. Blackspire's lords hold the Wyrm's beasts away from the Vales. The beasts go elsewhere.
  2. The Vales pay a yearly tribute. The royal house collects it from the outer towns and calls it the **Warden's Due** (grain, steel, silver).
  3. When the blood of the Smith-Queens wakes in the royal line, the house hands that heir to Blackspire. The Forge must never be lit again.
- Aldric the First married into the old Smith-Queen line. The blood slept in his family for two hundred years. The family kept the secret and the wheat grew. "Two hundred years of wheat, wool and dull letters" is the king's own phrase for the price.
- The Due was sold to the outer towns as a temporary war levy "until the Long Dark ends". It was never lifted.

### Hollowbrook, the brook and the cellar

- The brook comes from a hollow in the Old Wood. The old inn's cellar has a cold draught. It comes from a sealed dwarf door in the cellar wall. This is the secret door through which the Black Wing visited the king for years. The rats there grow big because of the cold. (Ch1 seed, ch9 payoff.)
- The king posted the newest, most innocent knight (Tobin) at that cellar door on purpose. Nobody would suspect him.

### The night Elowen was taken

- Elowen was sixteen. She found the torn strip of the Founding Tapestry in the archive. It shows how the Forge is lit again. Her blood woke: the ember bell rang with no hand on the rope.
- Her father knew what the bell meant. Clause three. He did not sleep that week. At midsummer he had the north tower window unlatched and posted the guards away from the stair.
- Elowen heard the plan through her mother's door, or guessed it from her father's face. She **barred her door from the inside** against the guards who came for her. The **Black Wing** (Corvane, the Dread Lord's herald) came through the window. She fought. The glass broke outward.
- That is why the door was barred from inside, the window broken outward, and the thing "knew which window". Someone told it. The king did. The captain's short list of names should have held one name more.

### The Warden's Due and what it did

- The Due fell hardest on poor hamlets near the Downs. **Thornmere**, the Smith's home, lost its seed grain in the hungry year. Thirty-one households were on the ledger. Few lived through the winter. The Smith's teacher took the Smith in. (Ch7 reveal.)
- The king's steward, **Corwin**, keeps two ledgers. One is called "Elowen's Return". The other is the real one. Corwin skims the Due and stores the surplus in an ice-vault in Frostmere to sell high in bad years. (Ch6)

## 4. Cast

Keep these people the same in every scene. The voice notes are rules.

### The Smith (the player)

- Seventeen. From **Thornmere**, a hamlet on the edge of the Barrow Downs, now empty. Not a hero, not chosen. Good with fire, slow to trust at first, fast to love.
- The player picks the name. The story never states the gender. It says "the Smith" and "you".
- Taught by **Brannoch**, a wandering tinker-smith who took the Smith in after Thornmere starved and died of cold on the road to Hollowbrook. His last words: "Find honest work, kid. The Vales are kind." He was wrong, and he did not know it. His saying: "Steel does not lie. The one who buys it might."
- Carries Brannoch's old hammer. The hammer is plain. It is not a relic. (Dagna says so in ch8: the fire answers the hand, not the hammer.)
- Voice: short, dry, honest. Starts polite and a little eager. After ch7 it is quiet. After ch9 it is quick, teasing, easy.
- What the Smith wants: to be useful, to be somewhere safe. What the Smith needs: to build a place inside themselves that does not need the world to be good.

### The Company (the player's heroes)

- Mercenaries hired in tavern after tavern. They have no names in the story, because the player owns them. Scenes say "your heroes" or "the Company".
- The Company is honestly greedy. They say "We want the pay, and we like you. Both are true." They never lie about it. They are the Smith's model of how a person can be selfish and loyal.
- The Smith pays on time, repairs gear first, and never sends a hero out in rough kit. This is the Smith's early creed, before the Smith can name it.

### Elowen

- Princess of the Vales. Sixteen at the abduction, seventeen at the end. Her mother's hair, her father's stubborn chin. Smiles when the painter leaves.
- Smart, playful, brave without being reckless. Fights fear with jokes. **Sings when afraid** and does not stop. Kind to servants (the honey cakes for a kitchen girl). Notices everything.
- Not a damsel. Barred her own door. Picks locks. Befriends guards. Keeps a running tally of insults for her captors.
- She knows her father sold her. She does not hate him loudly. She says: "He is a coward, and I am his daughter, and I got the better half."
- Calls the Smith **"Sparks"**. The Smith calls her "Your Highness" at first, then "Princess" (teasing), and says "Elowen" only at important moments.
- Voice: quick, warm, a little vain about her wit. Hides pain in a joke, then drops the joke when it matters. Never cruel. Never helpless.
- Motifs: the white ribbon (good morning), the swinging feet, honey cake, singing off key on purpose.

### King Aldric the Patient

- Warm, self-mocking, tired. "Do not kneel, because my knees hurt enough for both of us." Remembers names. Gives gifts. Sends kind letters.
- He loves his daughter, in the way a coward loves: tenderly, and not enough to pay for it. He believes he made the only sane trade. He tells himself that a thousand people eat because of one girl.
- His evil is comfort and fear. He never shouts. His letters grow warmer as his lies grow bigger.
- He says "patient" about himself, and means "I wait for others to bleed".
- Never write him as a snarling villain. Write him as a man you would like.

### Queen Isolde

- Sleepless, gentle, sharp underneath. Ties a white ribbon every dawn. She suspected, and chose not to know. This is her sin and she carries it.
- In ch8 she does one brave thing: she steals the torn strip of tapestry from the king's chest and sends it to the Smith with Pip.

### The people around the king

| Person | Surface | Truth |
|---|---|---|
| **Steward Corwin** | Fussy, funny about the thin treasury | Keeps the real ledger and the Frostmere ice-vault. Sells grain high. |
| **Sir Berric the Grey** | Thirty years, never lost a post | Obeys. Leads the knights who collect the Due and burn debtors. "Cannot leave the walls" was a lie. |
| **Bishop Orsen** | Gentle, prays for Elowen | Sells blessings. Pulled the priests from the Downs. Preaches against the Smith when asked. |
| **Chronicler Wynn** | Writes the endings last | Archived the Treaty, was told to burn it, kept it in memory. Never lies, never tells. |
| **Captain Edda** | Honest, keeps a short list of names | Loyal to the walls. Keeps the gate in ch10. The one who sends the ribbon news. |
| **Sir Tobin the Bold** | Newest knight, bright | Was posted to the cellar door as a decoy. Learns it in ch9. Opens the gate in the epilogue. |
| **Pip the Fool** | Jokes, saw the winged thing leave | Told the king, was told fools lie, and went quiet to keep his bread. Carries the queen's letter in ch8. Gets his courage back. |
| **Marta Pell** | Innkeeper, gives soup | Truly kind, and also takes the tithe-free butter. Proof that goodness and greed live in one person. |

### Recurring on the road

- **Vessa Roane** (ch3 to ch6): ex-knight, captain of her own small band. Deserted when Berric ordered her to burn a village. Tells the Smith the truth in ch3. The Smith does not listen. Fights beside the Smith in ch5. Dies in ch6 holding the Frostmere vault door against Berric. Her death is what the Smith cannot forgive in ch7.
- **Dagna Ironvein** (ch8): master smith of Emberhold. A broad, gruff dwarf-line smith. Sells to every side. "Steel is honest. Buyers are buyers."
- **Old Borin Stonehand** (ch4): the last dwarf-smith. Half deaf, half stone. Tells the Forge's secret in a riddle.
- **Corvane, the Black Wing** (ch1 felt, ch9 seen): the Dread Lord's herald. A great winged shade with a raven's mask. Polite and cold. Calls Elowen "the guest".
- **Lord Malachar Vey, the Dread Lord** (ch9): Blackspire's warlord. Loyal to the Wyrm out of fear, not love. Wants the treaty kept, so that his own fear stays small.
- **The Wyrm** (ch10): old, patient, amused. It does not rage. It offers. It is the patience the king only pretends to have.

## 5. Elowen's embers (the voice in the fire)

Every Workshop stands on an old dwarf-hearth, a stone that is still tied to the Ember Forge. Elowen's blood is the Forge's last living ember. When the Smith lights a hearth, a spark sometimes carries her voice. At first she cannot hear the Smith. She talks to the dark to keep her mind sharp. Her words are the only warm thing the Smith finds on the road.

- From ch1 onward the hearth speaks when the Smith finishes a boss. One short entry per chapter, in a small ember pop-up.
- In ch8 the line becomes two-way. After that, short banter lines can play at the hearth.
- Her entries are always funny first and honest last.

Voice samples (final text can differ, but the voice must stay):

| Chapter | Entry |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Is anyone there? If you are a rat, please be a polite rat." |
| 2 | "Day thirty. The guard learned that my name is not 'girl'. He is learning slowly. I am very patient. I am my father's daughter, which is the first time I have meant that as a threat." |
| 3 | "They bring me soup that tastes like regret. I told the cook, and she cried, and now I feel terrible. I will be nicer to the soup." |
| 4 | "I hum, and the stones hum back. I do not think that is normal. I think it is rude of them not to say more." |
| 5 | "A new window today, a small one. I can see snow. I have decided it is applause." |
| 6 | "My feet are cold. My mother used to say the cold is only the world reminding you that you are alive. Mother was wrong about many things, but she was right about that." |
| 7 | "I keep thinking someone is coming. I know that is foolish. I decided to be foolish on purpose, because it is better company than sense." |
| 8 | "You can hear me. Oh. Oh, say something. Anything. Do not say 'hello'. Everyone says 'hello'." |
| 9 | "You are late. Also, you are shorter than your voice." |
| 10 | "Sparks. Look at the sky. It is not dark. Was it always that colour?" |

## 6. The ten chapters

Each chapter lists: where and when, what the player does, the story beats, the boss, the king's letter, Elowen's ember, and the closing image. The Smith's belief is given at the start and the end.

### Chapter 1 - The Haven (Hollowbrook, levels 1-10)

**Belief:** full. **Boss:** Goblin Chief. **Season:** nine nights after midsummer.

The Smith arrived in Hollowbrook one season ago, starving, with a dead teacher's hammer. Marta Pell gave soup. The king's steward found the Smith a small workshop on an old dwarf-hearth. The people here are kind. The farms shine. The king knows the Smith's name.

Princess Elowen was taken nine nights ago. King Aldric receives the Smith in the Throne Hall and asks for the only help left: a smith who arms brave heroes. He promises that the crown will not be the only thing he gives. The Smith does not want a crown. The Smith wants to be worth the soup.

**Beats:**
- The tavern, the first hero, the Rat Cellar. Sir Tobin guards the cellar door and complains about the cold draught. (Seed for ch9.)
- The castle tales: the queen's ribbon, the jester who saw the thing leave, the captain's short list of names, the chronicler's blood guess. All warm. Every one of them is also a clue.
- The Scarecrow Field, the Wolf Trail, the Sunken Mill, the Goblin Camp, the Old Wood Hollow. The Company grows. The Smith learns the first craft.
- The Goblin Chief's Lair. The Chief is crude and tired. Dying, he spits: "Tell your king the silver stopped." In his chest of loot are sacks of Hollowbrook grain stamped with the royal seal, and a pouch of the king's silver pennies. The Smith decides the goblins robbed a wagon.
- **Closing image:** the king pins a white ribbon on the Smith's sleeve and says "Go east." That night, the hearth pops. A voice: "Is anyone there? If you are a rat, please be a polite rat."

**Seeds planted:** the cellar draught, the royal silver in the goblin lair, the Chief's last words, the captain's list.

### Chapter 2 - The Gate of Mounds (Barrowgate, levels 11-20)

**Belief:** high. **Boss:** Barrow Lord. **Season:** late summer.

The first town outside the Vales is poor, proud and wary. Reeve **Hald Marrow** will not sell the Smith grain at a fair price. The Smith is hurt. Hollowbrook's farmers would have given it free. The Smith thinks Barrowgate is stingy.

Barrowgate has had no priest for years. The Downs' dead do not rest. Skeletons, wights and clay golems walk the mounds. The Reeve's daughter **Wren** gives the Smith a small bone charm for luck, the only free thing in the town.

**Beats:**
- The Smith hears the Warden's Due named for the first time. Carts of grain with royal banners roll west while Barrowgate's granary stands empty. The Smith decides it is protection money for the knights who guard the Vales, and the Vales guard everyone.
- A short detour: Thornmere, two days north. The fields are grey and empty. Brannoch's grave is a pile of stones. The Smith lays down the bone charm. The Smith believes the Downs killed Thornmere. Nobody corrects this.
- The Barrow Lord's tomb. He was an old lord who buried his gold with himself while his people starved. The wights are the hungry dead, guarding his hoard. This is the first mirror: a man who hoarded and called it honour.
- The Smith writes to the king asking for knights for Barrowgate. The reply is warm and says "Soon."

**King's letter:** "Dear friend, I weep for the Downs. Soon, soon. Our knights are bound to the walls, and I am bound to my chair. Be patient, as I am patient."

**Elowen's ember:** the guard learns her name.

**Closing image:** the Reeve says: "Tell your king we are still here." The Smith promises to. The Smith sends the message, and the letter says "Soon".

### Chapter 3 - Stilts and Lamps (Mirewatch, levels 21-30)

**Belief:** high, with a bruise. **Boss:** Bog Hag. **Season:** autumn.

A town on stilts in the fog. Everything has a price: oil for the lamps, a guide through the bog, a dry bed, a dry word. The Lampwarden **Ilsabet Thorne** holds the oil and sets the price.

**Vessa Roane** is introduced here, resting with her small band. She says what nobody else says: "Ask where the grain goes. Ask why a king with an army sits still." The Smith bristles. Vessa shrugs: "You are very young, and it is very kind of you."

**Beats:**
- The guide **Sedge** leads the Company into a sink-bog and takes their purse. The Smith is hurt in body and in trust. The Company is angry. The Smith says: "A few bad people." Vessa says: "Count them again at the next town."
- Boss: the Bog Hag, Nan Gloam. She does not fight like a monster. She bargains: gold for a clear road, memory for a safe night. She has been paid for years by a royal agent to keep the fog thick on the west road, so that no one in the east can walk home and ask questions. "Even the fog is paid for, child. By your king."
- The Smith does not believe her. They kill her anyway, because she attacks. After, the Smith finds a ledger entry with Corwin's seal. The Smith burns it unread. The Smith does not look at the ash.

**King's letter:** a gift of fine steel, "for my friend who fights so far from me".

**Elowen's ember:** the soup that tastes like regret.

**Closing image:** the fog does not lift. The Smith lights the lamp and keeps walking.

### Chapter 4 - The Cold Hearth (Deepdelve, levels 31-40)

**Belief:** shaken. **Boss:** Troll Chieftain. **Season:** late autumn.

A dwarf mine town, abandoned in the Long Dark. A human **Mining Guild** (Guildmaster **Orvel Cragg**) now runs it and sells the dwarves' relics: hearth-stones, tapestry scraps, runed hammers. Nothing here is sacred. All of it is priced.

**Beats:**
- The Smith finds **Old Borin Stonehand**, the last dwarf-smith, half deaf, half stone, sitting by a cold forge. He speaks in riddles: "A fire is three things. A hand that is nobody's. A hammer that is anybody's. A song that is hers." He says the fire was never lost. It was hidden in a girl and a family. He says he did not hide it well enough.
- The Smith's hammer rings at the old hearth. Borin laughs: "Any hammer will do."
- The Troll Chieftain holds the mine's deep forge. He is not evil. He is hungry, and the Guild locked him out of the only warm place in the mountains. The Smith kills him. The Smith feels the weight of it more than usual.
- On the road out, the Company sees **Sir Berric's knights** escorting an unmarked cart toward the east. The same banners that "cannot leave the walls". The Smith says: "They must be on a mission." Nobody in the Company answers.

**King's letter:** longer, warmer. He asks if the Smith is eating well.

**Elowen's ember:** the stones hum back.

**Closing image:** Borin presses a cold hearth-stone into the Smith's hand and says: "When you are cold enough, it will remember."

### Chapter 5 - The Pass (Highpass, levels 41-50)

**Belief:** cracked. **Boss:** Orc Warlord. **Season:** first snow.

A fort in the high pass between the western Vales and the east. Every army crossed here and left bones. **Castellan Roderic Hale** holds the fort. He is polished, brave in speeches, and hires out his gate to whoever pays.

The Orc Warlord, **Hakkar Redtusk**, is the Castellan's enemy. Everyone says the orcs came down from the north to plunder.

**Beats:**
- The Company fights with Vessa's band for the first time. She is fierce and funny. The Smith likes her more than the Smith admits.
- The truth: the orcs were the fort's hired garrison. Hale stopped paying them when the winter came, sold their winter rations to Blackspire's agents, and locked the gate. The orcs did not invade. They were left to die and came back to ask for their pay.
- The Smith kills the Warlord. He dies saying: "We kept the pass for a king we never saw." The Smith has no answer.
- Vessa tells her story at the fire: Berric ordered her to burn a village over a missed payment. She did the first house and quit. "It was not one bad day. It was a hundred days I let go by."
- The Smith asks: "If the monsters were wronged, who were the monsters?" Vessa says: "Ask the next question."

**King's letter:** two lines this time. "Do not trouble yourself about the pass. The Castellan is a loyal man."

**Elowen's ember:** the small window, the snow like applause.

**Closing image:** the Smith looks at the Company's gear laid out in rows, every piece honest, and feels for the first time that it is the only thing that is.

### Chapter 6 - The Ice Vault (Frostmere, levels 51-60)

**Belief:** broken. **Boss:** Frost Giant. **Season:** deep winter.

A cold lake town in the far north. The people trade furs for steel and tell tales of a giant under the ice. Frostmere starves. The Burgomaster **Hjalmar Brandt** shuts the gates against the poor. Fuel is hoarded. Bread is hoarded. Nobody shares.

**Beats:**
- The Smith learns the giant is a guard, not a beast. He is bound by an old king's seal to stand in front of a vault under the ice. Berric is also in town, with a company of knights, on "the king's errand".
- Vessa's band and the Company break the vault. Inside: a mountain of grain, years of the Warden's Due, rotting in the dark, stored for sale in bad years. A ledger marked "Corwin" lies on the top sack. It is not Elowen's Return. It is the real one.
- The Smith opens the vault to the town. The poor eat. The Burgomaster and the guild take the rest. Everyone fights for the grain. The Smith's fair share rule lasts one hour.
- Berric arrives. He names the Smith a thief and a traitor, in the king's name. Vessa holds the vault door against him. She dies there. Berric does not enjoy it. He does not stop.
- The king's letter arrives by hawk.

**King's letter:** "My dear friend, there has been a misunderstanding. Return the grain and all is forgiven. A gifted smith should not be lost over a small mistake." The Smith reads it three times. Nothing in it is a lie, and nothing in it is true.

**Elowen's ember:** "My feet are cold. My mother used to say the cold is only the world reminding you that you are alive."

**Closing image:** the Smith carries Vessa's sword from the ice and gives it to the Company's best fighter. The Smith does not speak for the rest of the night.

### Chapter 7 - The Ledger of Sand (Sunscar, levels 61-70)

**Belief:** gone, then hate. **Boss:** Sand Tyrant. **Season:** the thaw.

A trade town built among the ruins of a forgotten kingdom. The caravans come here to sell what the Vales do not want to name. **Trade-Mistress Sahlen Doru** runs the great house. She sells the royal Due by the sack. The Smith is outlawed in the Vales now, so only the sand will deal with them.

**Beats:**
- In the trade-house archive, the Due Registers. The Smith looks for Thornmere and finds it. "Thornmere. Thirty-one households. Collected in full. Seed grain taken." Underneath, in Corwin's hand: "Second collection, spring. None remaining." The Smith counts the graves from memory. The numbers match.
- The ruins of the old kingdom tell the same story as the Vales. They traded their daughters to the Wyrm for four hundred years of quiet. Then the sand came. The Sand Tyrant, **Ammerak**, was their last king. He guards the vault of the treaty he signed. He speaks as the king would: "One life for a thousand. I counted it too."
- The Smith kills him. Nothing is felt. The Smith reads the last page of his treaty and understands the Quiet Treaty: the Vales' peace, the Due, the Wing, and the princess.
- The Smith sits in the dunes and says aloud: "I was the king's hammer. I forged the sword that killed my own people." The Company does not argue.
- The Smith stops. No more letters. No more crafting for a day. The hearth sings, and the Smith does not answer.

**King's letter:** the Smith does not open it.

**Elowen's ember:** "I keep thinking someone is coming. I know that is foolish. I decided to be foolish on purpose."

**Closing image:** the Smith turns the horse west. The Company stays still. They say: "We go where the work is, and the work is east. We would like you to come. We also would like to be paid." The Smith stares at them. Then laughs once, without any joy. It is the first laugh in six chapters.

### Chapter 8 - The Honest Fire (Emberhold, levels 71-80)

**Belief:** changed (acceptance). **Boss:** Magma Colossus. **Season:** spring.

A fort of black stone at the foot of a volcano. Smiths love it for the heat. Master smith **Dagna Ironvein** makes the best steel in the land. She sells to the Vales, to Blackspire, to the mercenaries, to anyone.

**Beats:**
- The Smith tells Dagna everything, bitterly. Dagna listens with the hammer in her hand. Then: "You think you are the first to find the world ugly? I sell to the men who will burn the buyers of the next lot. My steel does not care. My steel is straight."
- Dagna sees the old hammer and says: "Any hammer will do. Maelis was nobody too, before the fire." The Smith learns: the fire answers a hand, a hammer and a song, not a bloodline alone. It was never about destiny.
- **Pip the Fool** arrives, ragged, with a sealed letter from Queen Isolde and the missing strip of the Founding Tapestry, stolen from the king's chest. The queen writes: "I knew. I did not want to know. I am sorry. Please bring her home." The strip shows Maelis at the anvil with a girl who sings. The Forge needs both.
- The Company has its own talk. Each one says what they want: gold, glory, a roof, a pardon. All of it is selfish. All of it is honest. And they stayed. The Smith sees it. People can be both.
- The Magma Colossus guards the deep-forge of Emberhold, a hearth near the Ember. The Smith fights with a calm that is new.
- After, in the deep-forge, the hearth gives a different sound. It listens. The Smith speaks aloud for the first time: "Princess? If you can hear me, this is the nobody from Thornmere." A pause. Elowen: "You can hear me? Oh. Oh, say something. Anything. Do not say 'hello'."
- The first banter. It is short. It is the first time in a year the Smith smiles with no hurt behind it.
- The Smith forges one blade that night. Not for war. A plain sword with an honest edge. Dagna looks at it and nods. The Smith has a creed now: **Make true things. Pay fair. Leave when it is time. Do not ask the world to be good first.**

**Elowen's ember** (two-way): first real conversation.

**Closing image:** the Smith carves a small, plain mark on the sword's tang: a single flame. Dagna asks what it stands for. The Smith says: "That I was here, and I meant it."

### Chapter 9 - The Dark Fortress (Blackspire, levels 81-90)

**Belief:** opening. **Boss:** Dread Lord. **Season:** early summer.

The tower raised in one night. Black orcs, dread knights, necromancers. Nobody has been inside and returned, except the tribute carts.

**Beats:**
- The Company storms the lower walls in a long, hard series of fights. The Smith crafts through the night.
- Elowen is not waiting to be rescued. When the Smith reaches her cell, she has picked the lock, befriended two guards (with honey cake recipes), and drawn a map of the keep on the cell wall in charcoal. "You are late. Also, you are shorter than your voice."
- The banter is immediate. They tease each other down the corridors, between fights. She names the Company's heroes with nicknames. She tells the Smith the guard's real names. The Smith carries her small bag and finally laughs without effort.
- She tells the truth: the Treaty, the cellar door, her father's face that night, the barred door. She says it flat. Then she says: "I would like to hate him loudly. It would feel good. But I am tired, and I would rather spend the energy on you."
- **Corvane**, the Black Wing, attacks. Elowen sings. The Smith's hammer rings. They hold. Corvane retreats.
- The Dread Lord, **Malachar Vey**, in the black hall. He offers the Smith the Treaty again: "You can still keep the Vales safe. One girl for ten thousand." The Smith says: "I had a teacher who said steel does not lie." The Dread Lord falls.
- On the throne is a cold voice. The Wyrm speaks. "Little forge-thing. Bring her to my cliff. I will be gentle." Elowen answers: "You will not."
- **Tobin the Bold** arrives with a handful of loyal knights and a message from Captain Edda: the cellar door under the inn is open. Beasts are coming up from it. The Vales are no longer a haven. Tobin learned his post was a decoy. He cries. He asks if he can fight beside the Company. They say yes.

**King's letter:** none. The silence is the first honest thing from him.

**Closing image:** on the road out of Blackspire, Elowen takes the white ribbon off the Smith's sleeve, the one from chapter 1, and ties it around the hammer. "Good morning."

### Chapter 10 - The Edge of the Map (Worldsend, levels 91-100)

**Belief:** chosen. **Boss:** The Ancient Wyrm. **Season:** midsummer, one year after.

The cliffs, the abyss, demons, titans and dragons. The Ember Forge is a black anvil on a ledge above a burning pit. The Wyrm sleeps beneath it. The Long Dark is a cold that gets inside your coat.

**Beats:**
- The long walk, with the Company and Tobin and Pip. Elowen and the Smith talk about everything: soup, Thornmere, her little throne, the first thing each of them ever forged or broke. The tone is lighter than any chapter before.
- Elowen explains why people are selfish near the Wyrm: "It does not make them bad. It takes the curtains down." The Smith nods. "Then Hollowbrook just had better curtains." "Exactly. And my father was very fond of curtains."
- A hawk from Captain Edda reaches them at the last camp. The king has finally left the throne. He stands at the Hollowbrook gate with the knights who "could not leave the walls", and for the first time he defends the Vales from the thing he fed. It is too late for the treaty. It is not too late to be brave. Elowen reads it twice. She folds it. "Later," she says.
- The Wyrm wakes in the pit. It does not roar. It speaks. It offers: free me and I will keep the Vales; or bind me and watch them fall. "Everyone has a price. Name yours."
- The Smith says: "I want to be paid. I want the Company paid. I want the princess home. And I want to do this anyway."
- The fire is lit with three things. The Smith's hammer, struck true. Elowen's blood, one drop, no more. Her song, off key on purpose. The Wyrm is bound, not killed. It cannot be killed, because it feeds on what people are. It can only be kept asleep. A fire needs tending. It always did.

**Ending:**
- Dawn. The sky is the wrong colour, which turns out to be the right one. Elowen sits on the Forge's edge, swinging her feet, like on her little throne at six.
- She does not say "I love you". She says: "You know, you are the first person who ever came for me who did not want a crown." The Smith says: "I wanted to be paid." "Liar." "Fair."
- They agree to stay for a while, because a fire needs two: one to keep it, one to keep the keeper company.
- She sends a white ribbon to her mother by hawk, with three words: "Still moving. Home soon." Then, quieter, to the Smith: "I think I will like the next part."
- The last shot is the Forge's glow, the hammer hanging by the anvil with a white ribbon on its handle, and two shadows on the stone.

## 8. Running motifs and rules

- **The white ribbon:** the queen ties one every dawn (good morning). The king pins one on the Smith in ch1. Elowen moves it to the hammer in ch9. It is on the hammer at the end. Never use it for anything else.
- **The hammer:** plain, Brannoch's. It is never magic. The fire answers the hand.
- **Soup and honey cake:** small kindness. Marta's soup, Elowen's cakes for the kitchen girl, the guards' cake recipes. These show that goodness survives in small acts.
- **Curtains:** Elowen's word for the hiding of selfishness. Use only from ch9.
- **The brook:** water finds the low place. Use it for how things reach the poor last. Sparingly.
- **"Patient":** the king says it about himself. The Smith learns what it costs.
- **Singing:** Elowen sings when afraid. She sings badly on purpose when she is not.
- **No heroes die by name** except Vessa. The player's heroes are never killed in the story. The Company's losses in battle are not story deaths.
- **Greed is not only the king's.** Every town shows a version of it. The Company shows an honest version. Dagna shows a professional one. The point is not that greed is the enemy. The point is hiding it.
- **Never let a villain be stupid.** The king, the steward, the Dread Lord and the Wyrm all have a reason that sounds sensible. The player should half agree before they feel sick.
- **Never let the Smith be a chosen one.** Nobody says "you are the one". People say "any hammer will do".
- **No kiss, no confession.** The ending only hints. A look, a shared silence, a ribbon, a joke that lands softer than it should.

Style for game text: keep the lines short and plain, as the existing castle tales do. A person speaks in the voice from section 4. Names and places are written exactly as in this file.
